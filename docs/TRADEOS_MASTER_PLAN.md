# TradeOS Master Plan

**Status:** Strategic direction — requires owner approval before becoming operating policy  
**Owner:** 404 TradeOS leadership  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-07-14

## Executive thesis

404 TradeOS should not position itself as another web-design agency. Its strongest defensible position is as a **contractor technology company** that combines customer acquisition, digital operations, and purpose-built field-service software.

The company has two mutually reinforcing assets:

- **404 TradeOS** — the parent company, marketing site, lead-generation operation, implementation partner, and future customer-success organization.
- **TradeOS** — the contractor SaaS platform: CRM, cost book, estimating, proposals, contracts, invoices, jobs, scheduling, customer portal, and AI-assisted workflows.

The commercial advantage is the closed loop:

> 404 TradeOS helps contractors get found, capture demand, and improve conversion. TradeOS helps them estimate, sell, schedule, execute, bill, and learn from the work.

This is a stronger story than either “agency” or “field-service software” alone.

## North-star outcome

Build the most contractor-native growth and operations platform for small and midsize trade businesses, beginning with contractors that have outgrown disconnected spreadsheets and lightweight apps but are not ready for enterprise implementation costs.

The target customer is initially:

- 5–30 field technicians or a smaller firm with clear growth intent
- owner-led or operations-manager-led
- residential and light-commercial trades
- underdeveloped digital lead capture and follow-up
- inconsistent estimating, pricing, documentation, and scheduling
- needs measurable improvement without a six-month enterprise rollout

## What the company must become

404 TradeOS should evolve into five connected capabilities:

1. **Growth systems** — websites, local search, reviews, paid acquisition, call tracking, lead capture, and follow-up.
2. **Implementation services** — onboarding, data setup, workflow design, integrations, training, and process improvement.
3. **TradeOS SaaS** — recurring software revenue and operational lock-in through daily business use.
4. **Contractor intelligence** — cost, margin, conversion, capacity, and workflow insights derived from real activity.
5. **Customer success** — quarterly performance reviews, adoption coaching, expansion, referrals, and retention.

## Product and service boundaries

### 404 TradeOS owns

- parent-company brand
- demand generation and sales
- agency and implementation services
- customer onboarding and success
- marketing site and lead dashboard
- educational content and contractor diagnostics
- partner and referral network

### TradeOS owns

- contractor CRM
- cost book and estimating
- proposal, contract, invoice, and payment workflow
- project and job operations
- scheduling and technician assignments
- customer portal
- operational reporting
- AI-assisted estimating and future operational assistants

### Explicit non-goals for the near term

- becoming a general-purpose ERP
- building every payroll, accounting, fleet, and inventory feature internally
- competing with enterprise construction-management suites on breadth
- replacing mature accounting platforms before core field workflows are proven
- overloading the initial product with advanced dispatch optimization
- using AI as a novelty layer without measurable workflow value

## Strategic moat

The durable moat should come from the combination of:

- contractor-specific workflow depth
- proprietary cost and assembly knowledge
- real operational data from estimate through closeout
- implementation expertise gained through 404 TradeOS customers
- a unified acquisition-to-operations journey
- accumulated human-reviewed estimating decisions
- strong tenant isolation and auditability
- a recognizable design and interaction language

The moat is not simply “we use AI.” Competitors can add chat interfaces. The moat is a verified contractor knowledge system connected to real pricing, customer, project, job, and margin context.

## Revenue architecture

### Entry offers

- Contractor Digital Health Check
- Website and local-presence foundation
- conversion-focused landing page or website rebuild
- lead-response and review automation setup

### Core recurring offers

- website care and local visibility
- lead-engine subscription or managed growth service
- CRM and follow-up operations
- TradeOS software subscription
- reporting and quarterly success review

### Expansion offers

- AI-assisted estimating enablement
- data migration and custom cost-book build
- advanced dashboards and integrations
- customer portal rollout
- multi-location operations
- custom workflow and API work

### Commercial principle

Every offer should create a natural next step. No customer should finish a website project with no path into recurring value.

Recommended ladder:

1. **Get Found** — website, local visibility, reviews, tracking.
2. **Capture Demand** — calls, forms, missed-call response, pipeline.
3. **Run the Work** — TradeOS CRM, estimating, proposals, scheduling.
4. **Improve the Business** — analytics, automation, AI, quarterly reviews.

## Go-to-market strategy

### Phase 1 — local proof

- win and document several contractor customers in the Midwest
- produce real before/after results
- build repeatable onboarding
- refine offers around observable pain, not assumed features
- secure permission for screenshots, testimonials, and measured outcomes

### Phase 2 — vertical repeatability

Pick two or three trades where pricing, sales process, and field workflow are sufficiently similar. Build tailored pages, demos, onboarding templates, and cost-book packs.

Candidate starting verticals:

- plumbing/HVAC/electrical service
- remodeling and general contracting
- roofing, fencing, decks, landscaping, or excavation

Do not market equally to every trade until implementation patterns are proven.

### Phase 3 — software-led acquisition

- self-guided TradeOS product preview
- contractor diagnostic and ROI calculator
- guided trial or paid onboarding
- partner/referral program
- content built from real operational benchmarks

## Product strategy

### Current posture

TradeOS is in RC1 hardening. The immediate priority should be consistency, verification, deployment confidence, and contractor-facing polish rather than expanding into every adjacent feature.

### Near-term sequence

1. Normalize lifecycle behavior across persistence, API contracts, UI, and portal.
2. Close critical security, environment, and deployment-verification gaps.
3. Validate the lead-to-paid workflow with seeded and real contractor scenarios.
4. Add strong frontend test coverage around the highest-value workflows.
5. Run private beta with a small number of real contractors.
6. Capture adoption friction and simplify onboarding.
7. Add only the features that repeatedly block real customers.

### AI strategy

Build an **AI operations layer**, not a generic chatbot.

High-value sequence:

- scope extraction from notes, voice, and photos
- assembly and cost-item recommendations
- confidence and missing-information detection
- estimate review assistance
- margin and pricing warnings
- morning operations briefing
- schedule and capacity risk warnings
- customer follow-up drafting with human approval
- closeout and warranty documentation assistance

Every AI output should be explainable, auditable, tenant-scoped, and reviewable.

## Customer-experience principles

- contractors should understand the next action without training
- important workflows should use plain trade language
- mobile and tablet usability must be first-class
- status vocabulary must be consistent everywhere
- system-generated recommendations must show confidence and rationale
- sample or simulated information must always be labeled
- implementation should feel guided, not like configuring an ERP

## Decision filter

Before approving a major initiative, ask:

1. Does this improve contractor revenue, margin, speed, or control?
2. Does it strengthen the acquisition-to-operations loop?
3. Is it required by real customers or verified risk?
4. Can it be implemented without compromising usability or tenancy safety?
5. Will it create recurring value rather than one-time novelty?
6. Does it fit the current milestone?

If the answer is mostly no, defer it.
